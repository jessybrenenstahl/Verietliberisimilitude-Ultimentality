# Externality Without Extra-Symbolic Access

**A participant can be constrained by conditions it did not produce while accessing them through structured significance.** Symbolic mediation describes how a condition becomes available; causal provenance describes what produced it.

```text
symbolically mediated access ≠ causal self-enclosure
```

## Source and participation

Externality is relative to a specified participant and causal boundary. A condition originating outside that process can be received through a bodily, textual, computational, or other symbolic channel. Its mediated availability does not make it fictive or self-produced.

Origin, control, and prediction are different relations. A participant can predict an external event accurately without producing it. An internally generated result can be surprising. An external condition can support the participant's aim as well as resist it. Surprise and opposition may be useful observations in a case, but neither defines external provenance.

## Effective correction

Returned conditions can correct a model when they provide relevant information and can change its operation. A welcome result can confirm a scoped prediction; a conflicting result can reveal an error. The causal path and what the result establishes matter more than whether it feels disruptive.

Internal checking can also be corrective. A process may derive a consequence it had overlooked, compare its claims, or detect an inconsistency. This does not erase the distinction between an internal computation and an external observation. It establishes that more than one source can contribute to correction. [[witness-outside-the-ring|Effective corrective independence]] concerns whether relevant criticism can take effect, not a universal prohibition on self-assessment.

## Investigating provenance

Specify the process boundary, identify candidate causes, and trace how they affect the received event. Where possible, vary a source or channel and compare the result. Dependencies can constrain which variations are possible; [[causal-source-and-experiential-format|source and format]] remain different questions without being independently adjustable in every case.

If an event attributed to an external source is shown to be generated internally, revise that attribution. If a proposed provenance test cannot distinguish the cases it was meant to distinguish, improve the test. Neither result automatically eliminates externality as a useful causal relation.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, FT for the non-entailment from mediated access to self-production; causal attributions and operational tests require their own evidence.
