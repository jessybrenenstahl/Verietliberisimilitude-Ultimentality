# The Coupling Graph

**The Coupling Graph shows how participating terms regulate one another and what their organization makes possible.** It complements the [[decompression-map|Decompression Map]]: derivational dependencies say what a definition requires, while this view describes relations in operation.

## Reading a relation

`couples-with` identifies a composite and its participating terms. It does not specify a universal execution order, equal influence, or mirrored timing. `cross-regulates` identifies reciprocal constraints; `regulates` identifies a directed corrective relation. The source and target columns are identifiers, not a timetable.

The two affective compositions are **Love ⊕ Fear → Submission** and **Apology ⊕ Gratitude → Reconciliation**. [[submission|Submission]] organizes relation and boundary; [[reconciliation|Reconciliation]] joins inward correction with outward value. An object being reconciled is a different kind of relation from either component of that composition.

## Causal interpretation

A composition must identify how its terms contribute to the emergent operation. [[sequential-gating|A gate]] can connect a correction to an act that depends on it. That local direction does not prohibit feedback elsewhere, just as reciprocal regulation does not require equal strength or simultaneous action.

Error surfaces in the data describe failures under a specified loss of regulation. They are not diagnoses of a component merely because it operates independently. For example, ordinary Apology is not a guilt loop, and ordinary Gratitude is not the sentimental form.

The graph is effective as a reference when a changed relation changes its data row, visible table, and evidence links together. A link-valid graph can still misrepresent a source; its semantic fidelity remains a question of the actual relation represented.
