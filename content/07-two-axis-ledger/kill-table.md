# The Kill-Table

**The Kill-Table records selected claims, challenges that would change them, and procedures for checking the result.** It makes the [[falsification-standard|Falsification Standard]] concrete by connecting an objection to an actual target and warranted response. Its coverage is a maintained index, not a claim that every possible challenge has been enumerated.

## Candidate challenges and established outcomes

The [[the-2026-adversarial-run|2026 run account]] reports candidate challenges to four inventory closures. Their assessment requires the same level and definition as the target: an irreducible additional primitive, an exhibited emergent Force, a corruption outside the proposed taxonomy, or a needed function absent from SPLCW. A proposal described as “not obviously contained” does not yet establish irreducibility.

The [[open-proof-burden|Open Proof-Burden]] records incomplete completeness arguments. It does not automatically classify them as refuted. A demonstrated counterexample changes the claim it actually defeats; the utility and accuracy of the retained cases are evaluated separately.

## Standing review procedures

Beyond the four count-closures, the table records selected load-bearing claims in the corpus. Every row fixes four things: the *target page* whose claim is at stake, the *valid attack* that would refute it, the *expected change* the corpus must make if that attack lands, and the *review procedure* for checking whether the failure has recurred. A row is never a prediction that the claim is wrong; it is the precommitment that makes acceptance mean [[falsification-standard|survivable refutation]] rather than declared safety.

Each row is anchored to a two-axis ledger entry in the same way the headline closures are: a kill on the epistemic axis is a kill of the *claim as standing*, and any mapping the claim carried is scored separately on the alethic axis.

## Using the table

These rows describe review procedures, not a suite of automatically executed philosophical tests. A completed review should record its evidence and actual outcome. A hypothetical defeat condition is not a finding that the target has already failed.

The table itself can contain an inaccurate condition or an inappropriate expected change. Correct such a row against its target's meaning and the actual argument. [[canon|Source authority]] establishes intended usage; evidence and reasoning establish whether the described challenge succeeds.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, Exposition of selected review conditions. **A:** each row should accurately identify its target, relevant challenge, and warranted response. **Provenance:** maintained review guidance; candidate historical challenges are distinguished from demonstrated outcomes.
