# Correction Without Regress

## Definition

**Correction without regress** is the capacity to resolve an error and act without first certifying an infinite succession of correctors. The result, the rule used to reach it, and its application remain open to relevant new evidence. A completed correction is a real achievement; completion does not imply immunity from future correction.

The regress arises when permission to use any corrective rule depends on first certifying another rule, with no stopping condition. Recursive marking need not impose that dependency. It requires correctability, not completion of every possible future review before the present action.

[[formal-closure-claim|Semantic closure]] keeps correction and its evaluation within structured significance. That common domain does not by itself prove that a recursive procedure terminates. Termination needs a usable stopping rule: an established result, a sufficiently reduced error, or an explicit decision under remaining uncertainty. Where delay also has consequences, action can be warranted without a measurable error falling below a single numerical threshold.

```text
identify the error and the decision it affects
apply the relevant correction and assess the result
stop when the stated completion or action condition is met
retain a route for relevant new evidence to revise the result
```

## Type and formal status

**E: Derived, Mixed.** Inside the frame, correction remains within meaning and its formulations remain marked (FT). The proposed stopping procedure and its conditions are a revisable operational account (CV). Their compatibility must be shown; common membership in one semantic domain alone is not a termination proof. Internal criticism can test premises, inference, consistency, and application without declining the whole frame.

**A:** The account aims to describe actual correction that can finish and support action while preserving effective revision. Successful cases support that capacity within their demonstrated scope. The page does not certify every particular stop as well chosen.

## What it regulates

The account bounds paralysis caused by requiring an infinite sequence of prior approvals. It also bounds the demand that every resolved question remain actively reopened simply because further error is possible. Marking preserves a capacity; it does not require endless execution of review.

## What regulates it

- [[semantic-closure-and-recursive-marking|Closure and marking]] keep the result within the same domain and expose its formulation to criticism.
- [[causal-error-mark|Causal force]] makes reopenability effective: relevant new evidence must be capable of changing the account or operation.
- Explicit completion criteria and evidence distinguish a resolved error from work merely abandoned. The criteria themselves remain assessable.

## Valid attack surface

Challenge an asserted stopping rule by showing that it cannot be met, does not authorize the claimed action, hides an exempt judge, or fails to preserve effective revision. A practical limit matters when the page claims practical usability. A failure of one procedure calls for repairing that procedure; it does not establish that every correction requires an extra-symbolic certifier.

## What happens if isolated

A demand for prior certification at every level can generate regress. A stopping rule made immune to relevant criticism can close correction. Neither failure follows merely from omitting a label or failing to state the whole framework. Identify the operative dependency or blocked update path.

## What larger property emerges from the coupling

The coupling supports decisive, finite action with retained answerability. A correction episode can finish; the system can preserve its result and act on it. [[dynamic-fixed-point|Continuity through revision]] includes stable intervals in which no change is warranted.

## What would actually kill the claim

The general capacity is challenged by showing that usable stopping and effective reopenability cannot coexist under the stated conditions. A counterexample to a particular completion test defeats that test at its claimed scope. An argument that shared semantic type guarantees termination fails if it lacks a stopping argument; this page does not rely on that inference.

## Prohibited misreadings

- **Completion as infallibility.** A resolved error need not remain unresolved to remain correctable.
- **Reopenability as compulsory repetition.** Reassessment responds to evidence, changed conditions, or a justified review need.
- **A common domain as a termination proof.** Semantic closure locates correction; a stopping rule governs its execution.
- **Action as proof of adequate correction.** A process can stop too early. Inspect its criteria and evidence.
