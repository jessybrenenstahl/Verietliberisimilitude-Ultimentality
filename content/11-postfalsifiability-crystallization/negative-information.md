# Negative Information

## Definition

Within the framework's account of correction, **negative information** is selected absence: an exclusion whose reason is retained as a constraint on reconstruction or action. The absent alternative and the reason for excluding it jointly help determine the present structure.

An exclusion can be warranted by an observed failure, a demonstrated contradiction, a derivation from accepted conditions, or a deliberately specified scope. It need not first have been physically implemented and failed. Distinguish these grounds; a scope decision is not a refutation of everything outside that scope.

```text
excluded alternative + grounds + retained constraint
    → a reconstructible distinction
```

This is a specific use of “negative information,” not an exhaustive theory of absence as evidence. An expected observation that does not occur may also be informative under an established expectation and observation procedure. Bare silence alone does not establish an intended prohibition or a prior defeat.

## Type and formal status

**E: Derived, CV.** The selected-absence formulation is a revisable description of retained exclusions.

**A:** It aims to distinguish exclusions supported by stated grounds from gaps to which a reader has attributed an unsupported reason. A claim that an alternative was tested requires evidence of that test; a deductive exclusion requires its argument.

## What it regulates

The concept preserves the constraints needed to reconstruct a framework. Copying positive assertions while dropping their distinctions can change the framework's meaning. It also prevents an interpreter from filling every silence with an invented doctrine.

## What regulates it

[[scar-record|The scar record]] supplies reasons and relevant provenance. [[causal-falsifiability|Causal tracing]] checks claims about implemented correction. [[same-level-attack-rule|Scope-matched examination]] connects an excluded alternative to the claim it challenges. [[carving-tier|Revisability]] permits obsolete or mistaken exclusions to be removed.

Present function and historical origin remain distinct. A current prohibition can retain its reason and link to the source that established it. History is not excluded merely because its account is symbolically mediated.

## Valid attack surface

Show that a claimed test never occurred, an exclusion does not follow from its stated premises, a scope choice is falsely presented as a refutation, or a retained constraint no longer protects its claimed function. Missing evidence leaves a historical attribution unverified; it does not automatically make the gap accidental.

## What happens if isolated

Reading an unexplained absence as a settled exclusion can invent structure. Discarding a justified exclusion can erase structure. Neither a positive inventory nor a negative inventory alone establishes that reconstruction preserves the relevant dependencies.

## What larger property emerges from the coupling

The corpus carries both its assertions and its warranted exclusions in an inspectable form. This supports [[hypercompression|reconstruction from compressed forms]], [[crystallization|stable refinement]], and [[postfalsifiability|retained correction]] without making every prohibition permanent.

## What would actually kill the claim

An attribution fails where its stated grounds are false or do not justify the exclusion. The general model requires revision where it cannot distinguish warranted exclusions from unsupported readings of silence. An alternate account can improve the formulation without denying that constraints carry information.

## Prohibited misreadings

- **Every omission as doctrine:** establish its grounds.
- **Every exclusion as an experimental defeat:** derivation and scope also matter.
- **A failed alternative as universal proof of its rival:** assess the alternatives and test conditions.
- **A retained exclusion as emptiness or Nihil:** it is a constraint doing work, distinct from unexperienced structured signification.
