# The Sentimental Form

**The sentimental form is apparent reconciliation that leaves the relevant self-model error active.** Outward value or signs of peace stand in for correction, so subsequent conduct continues to reproduce the discrepancy.

## What goes wrong

[[reconciliation|Reconciliation]] couples [[apology|Apology]] with [[gratitude|Gratitude]]. In the sentimental form, the outward operation is treated as sufficient while inward correction loses causal effect. For example, a conciliatory gesture can conceal a mistaken account of responsibility that continues to govern conduct.

The failure is substitution: an appearance of repair takes the place of the correction the relation requires. Warmth is not itself the defect. Peace, pleasure, and generosity can accompany effective reconciliation; they become misleading when treated as sufficient evidence that the operative error has changed.

## Distinguishing the failure

Ask which discrepancy remained, how it continued to govern conduct, and what the apparent repair did instead of correcting it. A description that identifies no remaining error has not established this failure.

Standalone Gratitude is not failed Reconciliation. Nor does receiving value before identifying an error establish the sentimental form. The diagnostic concerns whether a reconciliation actually integrates the correction its object requires.

The complementary failure is a guilt loop: repetitive self-accusation that no longer improves the self-model or supports participation. It differs from successful [[apology|Apology]], which corrects the model, including unjustified self-blame.

## Repair

Restore the link between the relevant correction and outward conduct. Carry forward what remains valuable through the improved relation. [[sequential-gating|A gate]] can enforce a particular dependency, while the return of consequences keeps further correction available. The aim is renewed participation, not an obligatory performance of distress.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, AC for the named failure of Reconciliation; particular diagnoses depend on the discrepancy and conduct shown. **A:** maps apparent repair that leaves its governing error unchanged. **Provenance:** the framework's named pathology, distinguished from legitimate Gratitude and successful peace.
