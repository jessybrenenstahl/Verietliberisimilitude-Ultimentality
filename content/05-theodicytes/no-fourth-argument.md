# The No-Fourth Argument

**The no-fourth argument distinguishes failure of an aim from absolutization of a term in the meaning-act.** Within the self–world–medium decomposition, a missed aim does not by itself supply a fourth term or a fourth Theodicyte.

## The argument and its premises

The [[theodicytes|Theodicytes]] describe [[absolutization|absolutization]] of a term: [[spectre|Spectre]] concerns the world-relation, [[nephilim|Nephilim]] the self, and [[homunculus|Homunculus]] the medium. The relevant operation is a part taking the place of the whole.

1. A Theodicyte is a term-absolutization, not merely a missing result.
2. In this decomposition, the aim is what the terms serve; it is not an additional coordinate alongside self, world, and medium.
3. Failure to achieve that aim describes a missing result. It does not identify another term inflated into the whole.
4. Therefore that failure alone is not a fourth Theodicyte. Failure of handing-on is described as [[sterility|sterility]].

The conclusion follows at the stated level. It neither proves every possible failure is an absence nor makes pursuit of an aim immune to distortion. An imposed goal, captured channel, or harmful result still requires examining the actual process.

## Preserve the types

[[propagation|Propagation]] is a directional operator. [[gratitude|Gratitude]] binds it to received value. [[telos|Telos]] names answerable continuation. “Handing-on” can describe an aim in this argument, but that shorthand does not make the operator, binding, and ethically governed aim identical.

A transmission process can malfunction or be controlled by another process. Excluding failed aim from the term-absolutization list does not exclude those events from reality or from analysis. [[captured-propagation|Captured-Propagation]] records the limited category question rather than granting every propagation process an exemption.

## What remains to be established

The three named Theodicytes cover the three single-term positions in the stated decomposition. Whether that decomposition adequately covers a case, and whether other mechanisms require another level of analysis, remain substantive questions. [[installed-compulsion|Installed compulsion]] and the [[answerable-optimizer|answerable optimizer]] are candidate cases to examine through the [[corruption-stratification|stratification]]. Naming them does not itself prove or disprove an exhaustive taxonomy.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, FT for the conditional distinction under its explicit premises; CV for the decomposition's adequacy and any wider completeness claim. Internal incoherence or an invalid inference can challenge the conditional without requiring abandonment of the framework. **A:** distinguishes term-absolutization, missing result, and the mechanisms that produce a result. **Provenance:** the framework's no-fourth argument, scoped to kernel §6.21 and its three-term analysis.
