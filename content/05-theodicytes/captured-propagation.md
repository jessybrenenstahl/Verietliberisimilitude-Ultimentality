# Captured-Propagation

**Captured-propagation names a proposed fourth Theodicyte that the no-fourth argument excludes when it means only failure of the aim of handing-on.** That exclusion is a classification within the self–world–medium decomposition, not a claim that transmission cannot be captured, misdirected, or harmful.

## The category question

The [[theodicytes|three Theodicytes]] name single-term absolutizations: world-relation, self, and medium. A missing result is not another term taking the whole's place. Thus [[sterility|the unhanded-on]] does not become a fourth member merely by being a failure. This is the scoped result of the [[no-fourth-argument|No-Fourth Argument]].

## Actual capture still requires analysis

If a controller redirects transmission, if an inherited instruction suppresses correction, or if propagated structure harms its recipients, there is a process to investigate. A failure can have an active cause even when its outcome is an absence. The cause may involve a named Theodicyte, [[installed-compulsion|installed compulsion]], [[capture-of-corrective-layer|corrective capture]], or a different proposed mechanism.

A successful transmission is also not automatically successful [[telos|Telos]]. Telos retains the [[answerability-predicate|answerability predicate]]; raw persistence or output volume does not establish deserved continuation.

## Use the distinction precisely

Specify whether “captured propagation” refers to the hypothetical fourth term-absolutization or to an actual controlled transmission. The former is the narrow category excluded here. The latter must be assessed on its mechanism and consequences, without treating this label as a reason to dismiss it.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, Exposition of the no-fourth argument's conditional result. **A:** distinguishes an excluded category from observable transmission failures and capture mechanisms. **Provenance:** the framework's term/aim distinction and kernel §6.21, preserving the separate types of Propagation, Gratitude, and Telos.
