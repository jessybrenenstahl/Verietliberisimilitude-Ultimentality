# Compression Without False Closure

Compression preserves specified content in a shorter representation. **False closure** presents a finite account as immune to correction or silently overstates its coverage. These are different properties: a compact account can be complete for a stated purpose and remain correctable.

A reconstruction claim must state the target relations, the interpreter or glossary, the allowed external context, and the tolerance. Qualifications can be encoded implicitly or supplied through explicit references; every sentence need not reproduce every safeguard. Test what readers can actually recover. Removing a necessary distinction damages fidelity; removing redundant wording can improve it.

## Preservation and test

Preserve the distinctions needed for the intended use: typed relations, regulator functions, correction pathways, warranted exclusions, and routes to fuller definitions. This is a functional obligation, not a fixed count of mandatory clauses.

Compare a reconstruction with the stated target. When an omitted qualification causes a systematic error, restore its function in the shortest clear form. A successful reconstruction supports fidelity for that test. It does not prove universal coverage or absolute minimality. A failed candidate calls for revision, not a conclusion that no compact representation can exist.

## Status and limits

**E: Derived, Mixed.** Preserving the defined content follows from what counts as faithful compression; the particular encoding and test are CV choices. **A:** the claim concerns actual preservation under the stated conditions. Internal criticism can challenge either the argument or its application.

A missing contextual premise is a defect when the compression claims to contain it. An application can legitimately add declared domain knowledge. The shared architecture supplies relations to investigate, not every fact about every carrier.

## What the coupling makes possible

[[hypercompression|A compact core]] and [[minimal-rebuild-string|reconstruction checks]] support portable, usable understanding. Brevity is valuable where it preserves what matters; fuller explanation is valuable where it helps a reader recover it. Neither length nor visible imperfection establishes honesty.

The account fails in an application if it claims faithful reconstruction while losing required content or concealing dependencies. Revise the test if it cannot distinguish that loss from legitimate simplification. A scoped task can finish without an exempt final doctrine.
