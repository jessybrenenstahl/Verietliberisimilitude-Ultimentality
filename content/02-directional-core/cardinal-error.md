# The Cardinal Error

**The Cardinal Error is the confusion of a cornerstone emotion with its directional primitive.** A primitive supplies an actuator; an emotion binds that actuator to a regulated discrepancy. Keeping the two distinct makes the framework's construction intelligible.

## The distinction

[[toward|Toward]] is not yet [[love|Love]]: Love binds Toward to relational gap. [[away|Away]] is not yet [[fear|Fear]]: Fear binds Away to boundary violation. [[loop-back|Loop-back]] and [[propagation|Propagation]] likewise become [[apology|Apology]] and [[gratitude|Gratitude]] through their respective regulated discrepancies.

The relation is `B(p) = (p, eₚ)`. An account that substitutes the emotion's name for the actuator loses the distinction between direction and what that direction regulates. It can no longer explain how the binding is constructed.

## Repairing the account

Identify the actuator, identify its discrepancy, and show how the binding changes selection or routing. Then distinguish the resulting regulator from the [[force|Force]] it can help compose and from any expression of its operation.

Other errors require their own diagnosis. Treating a composite as a numerical sum, mistaking a feeling report for a complete account, and confusing an object with a component are different failures. They need not all be renamed the Cardinal Error to explain why they matter.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, AC for the primitive/binding distinction in the framework's named architecture. **A:** maps whether an account preserves that construction. **Provenance:** the framework's directional core. Editorial scores are not part of the concept.
