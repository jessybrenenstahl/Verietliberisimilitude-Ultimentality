# The Regulated Error Signal

**The regulated error signal `eₚ` identifies the discrepancy acted on by a binding.** It relates current conditions to a setpoint or admissible range and can change how the directional actuator operates.

## The four named discrepancies

| Binding | Regulated discrepancy |
|---|---|
| [[love|Love]] | relational gap |
| [[fear|Fear]] | boundary violation |
| [[apology|Apology]] | self-model error |
| [[gratitude|Gratitude]] | undischarged received value |

The actuator and its discrepancy together specify the [[predicate-binding|binding]]. Their causal organization matters: an error label that cannot affect selection, routing, or action does not establish regulation.

## What successful regulation does

A controller responds to the discrepancy under its actual constraints. It can reduce an error, hold it within bounds despite disturbances, or revise a response as conditions change. An exact numerical zero is not a universal prerequisite for effective participation.

The discrepancy is not a conserved stock. Regulation can preserve a relation or boundary precisely by changing the error and the response to it. This distinguishes continuity of a function from conservation of a signal.

## Evidence and expression

A word, gesture, bodily response, or other trace can express the operation. Its surface alone does not identify the binding. An account should specify the actuator, the discrepancy, and the consequential response rather than infer the whole process from a familiar expression.

Self-model correction is not synonymous with self-blame. Boundary regulation is not synonymous with a particular bodily symptom. Carrying value is not determined solely by a grateful utterance. The named discrepancy directs attention to the operation these expressions may enact.
