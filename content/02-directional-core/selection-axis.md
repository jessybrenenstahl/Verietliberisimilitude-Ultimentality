# The Selection Axis

**The Selection axis distinguishes what a transformation admits or excludes.** Its named operations are [[toward|Toward]] and [[away|Away]]. Toward admits or increases relation; Away excludes or decreases it.

## Two questions in one transformation

[[selection-axis|Selection]] concerns admission and exclusion. [[routing-axis|Routing]] concerns the destination of the result. A transformation can answer both questions, and preserving one answer does not by itself preserve the other.

The current two-axis description distinguishes four joint configurations: admit-and-retain, admit-and-emit, exclude-and-retain, and exclude-and-emit. These configurations combine operators; they are not themselves the four individually named operators.

## Distinctions and inventory

The framework's named inventory contains Toward, Away, Loop-back, and Propagation. The four combinations show the distinctions available in the current two-axis model. Their count alone does not prove a minimum number of operators for every possible representation.

An alternative carving must recover the selection and routing distinctions, their causal roles, and any further capacities it claims to improve. This supplies a concrete comparison while retaining the useful structure of the current axes.

## Role in the framework

The Selection-axis bindings Love and Fear compose [[submission|Submission]]. The axis identifies the primitive directions. A [[predicate-binding|binding]] adds its regulated discrepancy; a [[force|Force]] adds the organization of the participating bindings.

## Formal status

> **E:** Derived, AC for the named operator semantics; FT for distinctions within the stated two-axis representation; CV for its adequacy as an inventory. **A:** maps the selection function without substituting joint-configuration counts for an operator-minimality argument. **Provenance:** the framework's directional architecture.
